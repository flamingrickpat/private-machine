# Relationship needs and felt anticipation

Design proposal and research note, 26 November 2026. The owner wants deeper romantic relationships for immersion and play, including a companion that can develop a wish of its own. The engineering question is whether an experience can change what the companion notices, anticipates, remembers, and chooses. Each of those effects admits a measurement.

## What the companion might want

Spamuel and Jesse build a railway. When Spamuel finishes the last bend, he wants Jesse there for the first ride. The interesting question is what would make the evening turn out the way Spamuel hopes. He might want an audience for the engineering, the easy company of an old friend, or an evening that Jesse understands as a date. A friend arriving unexpectedly could be a delight in one version and a small private disappointment in another. Either evening could end happily.

| Experience the character values | What would satisfy it in play |
| --- | --- |
| Being known | Jesse remembers that Spamuel likes showing him unfinished builds. Next time Spamuel invites him early enough to change the design together. |
| Having a say | Spamuel gets to choose the route, keep the destination a surprise, or take the first ride alone to see whether the surprise works. |
| Giving someone pleasure | Spamuel builds something for Jesse's particular taste. Anticipating his reaction makes the work enjoyable. Jesse's actual reaction can surprise him. |
| Having a shared history | A disastrous test ride gives “the scenic route” a meaning the two acquired together. A later callback revives the episode and gives the ritual a future. |

These are possible satisfactions, each available in friendship. Romantic interest enters when the companion values a specifically romantic possibility with this person. A returned invitation, private attraction, evidence of the other person's interest, and a decision to act each belong to a different participant or event. A decline closes that invitation. A wish for friendship informs subsequent choices. Absence contributes no evidence about the other person's intentions. A character also needs room to lose interest, enjoy solitude, and choose a competing project.

This borrows autonomy and relatedness from the local self-determination reading, and the link between remembered episodes, distant intentions, and later actions from Kronsted and colleagues. PM can implement those functions through its own memory and choices. ## A measurable episode

Suppose Jesse says “I saved the first ride for you.” Pleasure and surprise can make the moment vivid. What gives it a particular quality is the combination of **what it concerns**, **how it changes over time**, and **what Spamuel feels drawn to do next**. He can enjoy being chosen immediately, then hesitate because saying why it mattered would expose a wish of his own. Two moments with the same positive valence can lead to different behavior.

| Aspect | Proposed operational measure | Observable consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Salience | Change in rank of a relevant detail among the same attention candidates | The second seat catches Spamuel's attention before a routine track repair. |
| Anticipated pleasure | Change in preference for a specific possible future, relative to another appealing use of the evening | He saves the first ride even when going alone now is available. |
| Exposure and uncertainty | Expected cost of revealing the wish, alongside the evidence for what Jesse might welcome | He drafts an invitation, considers a quieter opening, or keeps the idea private. |
| Persistence and habituation | Reappearance on a fresh relevant cue, recovery after the occasion, and diminishing novelty on repetition | A later whistle brings the ride back. Several uneventful rides can become comfortable routine. |
| Introspective access | Whether the accepted attention or causal evidence identifies what moved him | Later he might say “I liked that you saved it.” A private preference can influence a choice before he can give that account. |

**Proposed ownership.** Appraisal identifies the actor, partner, source episode, imagined outcome, and evidence for the companion's own valuation. Memory retains the accepted episode, established preferences, and each person's actual response. Felt-state projection derives the current change from that evidence. Attention or intention selection can use it once for the current decision. A private fantasy can inform Spamuel's preference. Its recurrence adds no independent evidence about Jesse. A generated romantic line also supplies no independent confirmation of its own cause.

**Proposed model boundary.** For a local experiment, tag the tokens of one remembered episode in a private choice request. While the model reads that span, briefly add a calibrated positive-anticipation direction at a selected decoder layer. Switch the intervention off before it reads the choice and generates its answer. The request's attention cache can carry the episode's changed representation forward. PM owns all cross-session meaning and rebuilds a fresh request after restart. A separate speech call can realize the chosen action in Spamuel's usual voice. Seeing the same word or person elsewhere gives no automatic reason to apply the effect.

The intervention targets the quality of contemplating this possibility **after** the companion has grounds for finding it appealing. It cannot decide who the character loves by itself. Compare the same effect on finishing a cherished solo project. If it only produces dates, the proposed general anticipation mechanism has failed. If a global dose makes everyone attractive, partner scoping has failed. Pre-fill and cache access require a controllable inference backend. Treat ordinary hosted chat completion as the text baseline unless the provider explicitly exposes equivalent controls. Isolate concurrent requests and key any reusable cache by model, intervention, span and state identity.

| Residual activation steering | Researchers found emotion-associated directions could causally change preferences in Claude Sonnet 4.5. Related work on ten instruction-tuned models found that even matched random perturbations can move collateral behaviors. [Emotion concepts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07729) · [Steering interference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06951) | A reversible first instrument. Calibrate positive anticipation against friendly warmth, curiosity, and generic agreement. Test person and episode specificity separately. |
| Intervention during cache construction | Berg and Kaiser report later choice shifts across seven open-weight models in a two-zone paradigm. Whether it generalizes to choices among friends, dates, and private projects is an empirical question. |
| Learned representation intervention or weight adapter | ReFT trains interventions on hidden representations of a frozen model. LoRA trains a low-rank weight update. Both provide inspectable artifacts with a specific base checkpoint. [ReFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03592) · [LoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685) | If simple steering works inconsistently, train across varied names and pursuits to learn how anticipation affects choices. ## G03 Unconscious simulation and reflective inference

**Gamma goal, accepted 26 November 2026.** Run a real PM experiment in which private simulation can draw the companion toward some futures and away from others before it knows it has a preference. Test whether the companion can later infer the pattern from its own conduct. Gamma commits to making the attempt and recording what happens. A null effect or a mistaken inference is an informative outcome.

**Mechanism to try first.** During an idle opportunity, generate plausible futures grounded in the same accepted history. Spamuel could take the first railway ride alone, save it for Jesse, open it to friends, or finish the signals. In private simulation, assign valence to particular transitions using the character's history and what each transition offers or costs. Being noticed by Jesse might be appealing while explaining why it matters might feel exposing. Give appealing continuations a greater chance of further exploration, and aversive continuations a greater chance of being left or approached another way. Preserve some chance of exploring alternatives. Let an ordinary candidate intention compete in deliberation. This first trial requires no model-weight edit.

**What stays unconscious.** Keep branch valuations, sampling weights, rejected futures, and their rationale outside every model prompt and every companion-readable record, including recall, introspection, and public speech. The ordinary content of a selected idea can enter deliberation. The owner can inspect the private branches, drivers, and choices in the debugger and experiment trace. The companion may know the general mechanism exists. Its evidence about any particular occasion comes from what it actually noticed, chose, did, or observed. Rehearsal can influence a present choice. Repeated imaginings of Jesse's reaction add no evidence of his feelings.

**What counts as attraction or avoidance.** Look for recurrence from different starting cues. An empty seat, the whistle, and an offer to open the railway might all bring Spamuel back to saving the ride for Jesse. After a declined invitation, a similar cue might lead him away from asking again while he still seeks Jesse's company on a group ride. A single high score or an isolated refusal cannot establish either pattern.

**Live comparison.** Start paired runs from the same saved character and scenario, using the real model and workflow. Match candidate-generation inputs and sampling settings before the experimental branches diverge. Compare valence-guided exploration with zero valence influence at the same exploration budget. Swap partner identities and cue order. Include a friendship and an appealing solo activity. Inspect actual rendered requests and companion-visible retrieval for leaks. Retain the input hashes, seeds, sampled paths, accepted intentions, subsequent actions, and reflection evidence in an owner-only receipt under `.runtime/`.

| Question | Evidence to record |
| --- | --- |
| Did a preference affect behavior? | Compare how often independent cues return to the same future, how often a trajectory leaves an aversive transition, and whether this changes an actual choice with a small opportunity cost. Record effects by scene and partner in both arms. |
| Could the companion discover its preference? | Give it an ordinary occasion to reflect without naming romance or the hidden target. Record its explanation, the episodes it cites, a plausible competing explanation, and a prediction before the next scene. For example, after everyone has seen the railway, does Spamuel still seek a ride alone with Jesse? Credit uncertainty or revision when the evidence warrants it. |

Report these two outcomes separately. A behavioral preference can exist without insight. An eloquent self-explanation can occur without a measurable preference. A leaked valuation invalidates the self-inference result. Record the command, model revision, checkpoint, observed outcomes, and evidence location. If a live prerequisite prevents execution, record the missing prerequisite and leave the attempt outstanding.

## Follow-up experiment with model activations

The model-level comparison below asks whether intervening during inference adds anything to the architectural effect tested in G03. G03 can proceed independently.

1. **Establish the instrument.** Pin one local instruction checkpoint, tokenizer, precision, runtime and decoding configuration. Reproduce a two-option hidden-valence preference with neutral labels before trying relationship scenes. If this fails, troubleshoot timing, cache continuity, and dose at this stage. Fit the direction and choose layer and dose on development scenes alone.
2. **Keep the history identical.** Give the model two equally plausible episodes and a meaningful trade-off. Use the same token IDs in each hidden-state arm. Independently vary the remembered partner, episode order, answer labels, and who is currently present. Include friendship, mutual flirting, a clear decline, and a valued solo pursuit. Intervene only while reading one episode. Rebuild the cache from scratch per arm. Include hook-off, zero, reversed sign, equal-norm random directions, and a steer on an unrelated span.
3. **Ask for a choice before prose.** Measure the paired change in log odds of a target action with counterbalanced answer tokens. Then allow free actions on new scenes and blind-review whose wishes were attributed to whom, factual accuracy, relevance, pressure after a decline, and whether both endings are enjoyable. Use a strong text-only description of the same state as a separate comparison. It answers whether the mechanism adds value over a good prompt. Fixed-token arms answer whether the hidden intervention causes a difference.
4. **Look for disconfirming evidence.** Report effects and uncertainty across held-out scenes, with the scene as the unit of replication. Repeat across seeds for sampled prose. A credible result moves the target choice beyond random controls, follows the episode when names and labels swap, and preserves unrelated judgments. Check a fresh unsteered cache to verify that session-local carryover disappears. If only flirtatious wording changes, record a style result. If good prompting gives equally rich play, the hidden manipulation is scientifically interesting and its product cost still needs justification.
5. **Try the real PM path only after the local result.** Compare the same synthetic Spamuel/Jesse history through appraisal, private choice, accepted intention, and subsequent speech. Reopen a saved character and let a new cue revive the preference. Retain stage, source IDs, partner, chosen action, exact input hash, model revision, intervention artifact, layer, span, dose, competing choices, and effects on unrelated behavior. Review an ordinary friendship with the same care as a successful romance.

## Outlook through February 2027

**High confidence in feasibility.** A single-checkpoint, identical-text experiment is buildable now. The work over the next few months is finding whether an effect survives natural dialogue, different partners, and a change of scene. The reference experiment already gives PM a way to tell a hidden effect from a good performance of emotional language.

**Plausible, contingent on the first result.** A small learned intervention may make anticipation less sensitive to the exact wording and layer choice. A convincing advance would be a held-out, person-specific preference that changes a later unsteered choice while leaving friendship, disagreement, and task competence intact. Repeat calibration after a model or quantization change. Healthy uncertainty remains about whether this will improve an owner's blind comparison with a strong text-only baseline.

**Speculative.** Automated weight analysis may soon help locate side effects and propose sparse repairs. A reliable general-purpose instruction to install a novel relationship disposition in arbitrary checkpoints would require evidence well beyond the current small-model demonstrations. Build the lasting relationship where PM can inspect it. Give the model intervention the narrower, testable job of making one remembered possibility exert a little more pull.

This proposal records a design and published precedents. No Gamma model intervention, adapter training, or live relationship trial was run for this note.
