## Consequences, commitments, and expectations

A committed thought is an executed internal event.
Its described possibility remains imagined or inferred.
Main appraisal can react to the thought's personal meaning.
A sad speculation can increase sadness without recording its imagined loss as fact.

Connect a longer commitment to an immediate goal and the selected action intent.
For example, a desire to repair a relationship can influence a later invitation or a choice to wait.
A desire is not automatically an accepted commitment.
Record the accepted choice and its reason.
Keep goal lifecycle and completion evidence in the existing goal owner.

When anticipation affects a choice, record the expectation before observing the outcome.
Link the later outcome to that expectation.
Distinguish unmet, contradicted, supported, and not-yet-observed expectations.
The absence of a user reply does not prove rejection.
The next appraisal can use surprise or disappointment without inventing intent.
A19 later uses these episodes for tested strategy changes.

## Recent workspace history and noticing

Read a bounded selection of recent committed workspace turns through existing memory contracts.
Keep their identities, order, shard, and timestamps.
Do not create another authoritative Conscious Contents Queue store.

A noticing operation compares current attention with active goals or recent interpretation patterns.
Its result is another proposal for attention.
It is not a privileged observer that edits state directly.
An accepted focus intention can alter the next relevant attention decision.

Distinguish inward attention from a workflow's execution stage.
A private loop can inspect a social event.
A main loop can attend to an inner concern.
Loop identity alone does not establish inward focus.

Rumination can preserve and reinforce negative affect.
Repeated retrieval of one source must not increase factual certainty as independent evidence.
Regulation can offer alternatives without forcing emotional resolution.
Record repeated causes, changed interpretations, and any deferred work.

## Disclosure and interruption
