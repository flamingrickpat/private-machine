# The second conscious loop

## Summary

The inner loop has its own GWT shard and private attention.
It plans, speculates, theorizes, and manages goals without a conversation partner.
Its accepted results influence main appraisal and attention.
The companion chooses which results become public actions.

Parent: [cognitive architecture](cognition.md).
Related: [self-model and reflection](reflection.md) and [A17](work-items/a17.md).

## The second conscious loop

A17 adds a parallel conscious workflow that can run without a new user message.
Triggers include unresolved goals, surprising outcomes, retrieval associations, needs, and idle opportunities.
A thought can compare possibilities, revisit a memory, or develop a private hypothesis.

The loop reads a versioned main GWT shard and its own inner-thought GWT shard.
Its attention codelets select more private knoxels and less outside material.
Its workflow can manage goals, theorize, plan, and perform meta-management.
These tasks do not require a conversation partner.

Each loop owns its attention frame and workspace publication.
Ghost commits preserve both shards and their causal references.
Concurrent work reads explicit revisions, then validates or refreshes stale proposals.
It must not overwrite the other loop's latest workspace.

Thoughts carry source links, uncertainty, and an experience-versus-imagination distinction.
They can change later attention or goals through validated proposals.
They are not automatically spoken aloud.

Accepted thoughts and artifacts return to the main loop with a self-origin event type.
The ordinary appraisal path processes their personal meaning.
Thinking about a sad possibility can therefore cause actual sadness in the simulated state.
The possibility remains attributed as speculation, rather than an experienced outside event.

An internal instruction to focus can nominate attention or revise an attention strategy.
This gives reflective thought influence over the more automatic main cycle.
Feedback depth, repeated causes, and stalled methods remain observable.
Regulation can respond to rumination without erasing negative emotion by default.

Sharing a thought is a chosen action.
The dialogue workflow receives its selected shard, mental landscape, and supporting evidence.
It can describe an idea naturally without exposing the complete private transcript.
An absence question can retrieve a time-bounded summary of actual private activity, including ten-hour intervals.
The companion can decide what to disclose or keep private.

These are explicit simulated mental events.
They do not expose the underlying language model's hidden inference process.
A stopped application has no executed activity to remember.
Startup/gap events still let main appraisal respond to the elapsed absence.

Background work yields to user interaction and urgent body control.
Persistent rumination can retain attention and reinforce affect over many cycles.
The scheduler uses cooldowns and summaries to control repeated inference without erasing that state.
Resource limits pause or defer work with an observable reason.
